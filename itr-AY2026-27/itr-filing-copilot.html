03 · Decision

Which ITR form?

Answer the questions and the right form falls out. The single most common mistake: not realising that intraday equity trading is "speculative business income", which forces ITR-3 even for tiny amounts.

Reference: the four individual forms

FormWho it fitsRules out
ITR-1 (Sahaj)Salary/pension + one house property + interest/dividends, total income ≤ ₹50 lakh. (Small 112A LTCG within the exempt limit is now tolerated.)Any business income, capital losses to carry forward, more than one house, foreign assets, director/unlisted shares
ITR-2Everything in ITR-1 plus capital gains/losses, multiple houses, foreign assets, >₹50 lakh incomeAny business or professional income — including intraday and F&O
ITR-3Anyone with business/professional income: intraday trading, F&O, freelancing with real books, plus all ITR-2 items— (it's the widest form)
ITR-4 (Sugam)Presumptive business/profession (44AD/44ADA/44AE) ≤ ₹50 lakh income, residentCapital gains beyond small exempt LTCG, loss carry-forward, intraday trading
i
Why intraday ≠ capital gains: Section 43(5) defines a trade settled without delivery as a speculative transaction. Intraday equity is exactly that, so its profit is business income, not capital gains — and business income means ITR-3 (or ITR-4 presumptive, but speculative income isn't eligible for presumptive treatment). Delivery-based selling stays capital gains even in the same account.
04 · Ground rules

Regime, rates & loss rules

Five minutes here prevents the worst mistakes: wrong regime answers, wrong due date, and losses silently lost forever.

New regime is the default (section 115BAC)

Slabs for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), normal income:

SlabRate

Above ₹24,00,00030%
  • Rebate u/s 87A: resident with total income ≤ ₹12 lakh pays zero tax on normal income (rebate up to ₹60,000, with marginal relief just above). The rebate does not apply against 112A LTCG.
  • Standard deduction ₹75,000 against salary. Almost no other deductions (no 80C/80D/HRA).
  • Health & education cess 4% on the tax.
!
The Form 10IEA trap (ITR-3/4 filers only): a business filer who wants the old regime must have filed Form 10IEA before the due date. If staying in the new regime, the portal still asks two 10IEA questions in Part A General — answer No to both ("filed 10IEA in earlier AY?" → No; "furnishing 10IEA for current AY?" → No). Leaving them blank throws a validation error.

Capital gains rates (post-23 Jul 2024)

TypeRate
STCG on listed equity/equity MF with STT (111A)20%
LTCG on listed equity/equity MF with STT (112A)12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh exempt
Listed equity holding period for "long-term"> 12 months

Speculative (intraday) profit is taxed at normal slab rates as business income.

Loss set-off & carry-forward

  • STCL sets off against any capital gain; carries forward 8 years (against capital gains only).
  • LTCL sets off against LTCG only; carries forward 8 years.
  • Speculative loss sets off only against speculative profit; carries forward 4 years.
  • Carry-forward is allowed only if the return is filed by the due date. This is the main reason to never file belated when there are losses.

Tax audit — and the due-date dropdown trap

Audit u/s 44AB applies only when business turnover exceeds ₹1 crore (₹10 crore when ≥95% of receipts and payments are digital). For intraday, "turnover" is the sum of absolute profits and losses — a small trader is nowhere near it.

!
Real error we hit: selecting 31st October as the due date made the portal demand a tax audit ("mandatory to have a tax audit under 44AB") and block upload. 31st October is the audit-case due date. A non-audit business filer must pick the earlier non-audit date — in 2026 the dropdown offered 31 Aug / 31 Oct / 30 Nov, and 31st August was correct.

07 · When the portal says no

Error fixer

Every validation error and portal quirk from the real filing, with the exact fix. Category A blocks upload; Category B/D lets you proceed. Type to filter.

Schedule 112A CSV upload fails blocking

common.errors.csv_row_skip

Cause: the CSV validator is unforgiving and the error never says why. Known fatal issues: writing the full text "After 31st January, 2018" instead of the code AE/BE in column 1a; unicode minus "−" in the header; commas/decimals in numbers; missing ISIN.

Fix: abandon the CSV. Use + Add Another to enter one consolidated row manually: 1a=AE, ISIN=INNOTREQUIRD, name=CONSOLIDATED, integer sale value and cost. Two minutes, works first time.

Due date forces a tax audit blocking

Since you have selected 31st October as due date, it is mandatory to have a tax audit under 44AB.

Cause: 31st October is the audit-case due date. Picking it tells the portal you're an audit case.

Fix: Part A General → change due date to the non-audit date (31st August in 2026; normally 31st July). Re-validate. No audit schedules needed.

A19(b) business-income question wrong or reset blocking

Please select Yes for 'Do you have income from business or profession for current AY' if you have any income from Business or Profession. 48. Income from specified business (46-47): Please select option as 'Yes' at Sl.no.A19(b)…

Cause: A19(b) in Part A General is "No" while Schedule BP has business income. These two errors always come as a pair. Beware: editing other Part A fields (like the due date) can silently reset A19(b) back to No.

Fix: Part A General → A19(b) = Yes → then answer the two Form 10IEA follow-ups (both No for new regime) → save. Re-check A19(b) after any later edit to Part A.

Form 10IEA questions left blank blocking

Please select option for have you filed form10IEA within due date for any earlier assessment year

Cause: setting A19(b)=Yes reveals two 10IEA questions that start unanswered.

Fix (staying in new regime): "filed 10IEA in earlier AY?" → No; "furnishing 10IEA for current AY?" → No. (Only a business filer switching to the old regime files 10IEA, and it must be before the due date.)

Secondary address question unanswered blocking

Kindly select the appropriate option for secondary address in Schedule Part A General

Fix: Part A General → Contact section → "Is the secondary address same as primary address?" → Yes (unless a genuinely different address is needed). Save.

Dividend quarterly breakup missing blocking

Quarterly breakup of dividend income should be equal to dividend income reduced in Schedule OS (row 3a)

Cause: Schedule OS has dividend > 0 but the five-column quarter table is empty or doesn't sum to the total.

Fix: fill the table (columns: up to 15/6 · 16/6–15/9 · 16/9–15/12 · 16/12–15/3 · 16/3–31/3). Equal split is legal when dates are unknown — use the . Cells must sum exactly to the dividend figure.

Dividend consistency between BP and OS warning — usually ignore

If you are required to prepare/maintain books of account and dividend income is reported in Profit & Loss Account, please ensure consistency… Please ignore if not applicable.

Cause: a generic cross-check for businesses that route dividends through their P&L.

Fix: none needed for a no-books filer whose dividends sit only in Schedule OS. The message itself says "ignore if not applicable" — click Proceed To Verification.

Expected due date not in the dropdown quirk

Dropdown shows only: 31st August · 31st October · 30th November

Cause: CBDT extended the year's deadlines; the classic 31st July option disappears in extension years.

Fix: pick the earliest listed date that isn't an audit date — that is the non-audit due date (31st August here). October = audit, November = transfer-pricing.

TDS shows odd small amounts (paise rounding) quirk

26AS shows TDS ₹14.78; portal row shows ₹15

Cause: the portal works in whole rupees and rounds each 26AS entry.

Fix: accept the pre-filled rounded figure. Also expect the deductor's gross amount to be only its own payout (e.g. ₹148 from one AMC), not your full dividend total — that's correct; the rest of the dividend simply had no TDS because each other payer stayed under the 194K per-payer threshold (₹10,000 from FY 2025-26).

Refund ≠ TDS paid quirk

Taxes paid ₹15 but refund shows ₹20

Cause: section 288B rounding — the final refund/demand is rounded to the nearest ₹10.

Fix: nothing. It's normal.

09 · Aftercare

After filing

The return processes on its own; here's what to expect and when to act.

Timelines

Processing (143(1) intimation)typically 3–6 weeks
Refund credit after processinga few days, direct to the pre-validated account
With minor queriesup to 2–3 months

Track: e-File → Income Tax Returns → View Filed Returns.

Keep forever (well, 8+ years)

  • Acknowledgement (ITR-V) + the filed JSON
  • Broker P&L reports and AIS PDF used
  • The loss carry-forward figures — next year's BFLA/CFL schedules will need them, per year, per loss type

If something was wrong after all

  • Revised return u/s 139(5): can be filed any number of times until 31 December of the AY — it fully replaces the original. Same process, but choose "Revised" and quote the original acknowledgement number.
  • 143(1) intimation with a demand: compare their computation column with yours; respond/pay or file rectification u/s 154 if they're wrong.
  • Defective-return notice u/s 139(9): respond within the stated window through e-Proceedings — usually the same fixes as the Error fixer section.

Next year's head start

  • Enter last year's carried-forward losses in Schedule CFL's earlier-year rows (with the original filing date) and watch BFLA set them off against any new gains.
  • Once a trader, the ITR-3 pattern repeats — this walkthrough stays valid; only amounts change.
  • If advance tax becomes relevant (tax liability ≥ ₹10,000), instalments fall due 15 Jun / 15 Sep / 15 Dec / 15 Mar.
